01 / WHAT COUNTS AS PERFORMANCE?

A retention result
isn’t a profit result.

The papers answer different questions. Some measure actual sales or productivity. Others measure turnover, rated performance or a task completed in an experiment.

That distinction changes what you can conclude. This atlas keeps those outcomes separate.

Explore workplace-output papers ↗

02 / THE IMPLEMENTATION MATTERS

“Offer mentoring”
isn’t one treatment.

In a sales organization, mandatory mentoring increased productivity. A voluntary version did not. Workers with more to gain were less likely to volunteer.

The result concerns the policy that assigns mentoring, as well as what mentoring does for participants.

03 / LOOK FOR THE UNCOMFORTABLE RESULT

A familiar practice
can backfire.

Public performance rankings reduced productivity in a sweater factory. Private rankings had no average effect.

In separate experiments, surprise bonuses also reduced output. Those experiments used temporary data-entry work—a material limit on generalization.

04 / ASK WHAT IDENTIFIES THE EFFECT

A method’s name
isn’t a guarantee.

A large care-sector study uses instrumental variables to estimate effects of HR bundles on customer growth.

Its instruments use local peers’ HR practices. Shared local demand or funding shocks could also affect growth, threatening the causal interpretation. More observations cannot repair that assumption.

05 / FOLLOW THE PAPER, NOT A SCORE

Find a practice.
Inspect its evidence.

Start with a practice and the outcome you care about. Check the treatment, setting, comparison and identification risks. Then go to the original paper.

There is no pooled “HR effect” here. These treatments, outcomes and populations do not share one estimand.

Scope & publication dates +

1 January 2025 through 18 October 2026. First journal online publication is preferred over issue year. Some dates have only an in-window issue/year verified; each study records that uncertainty.

68 retained papers, plus one candidate with an unresolved original-publication date. “Retained” means relevant to this broad inventory, not a high-certainty causal rating.

How the papers were found +

Crossref publication-date queries retrieved 9,600 metadata records across 49 selected journals. After removing obvious nonresearch items, 9,229 remained. A broad HR title filter and targeted causal-design searches guided triage; 107 unique candidates were individually assessed.

Publisher abstracts and metadata were the baseline; selected methods and author manuscripts were checked. This is a single-reviewer scoping inventory, not full independent screening of every abstract or paper. No licensed PsycINFO, Scopus, Web of Science or EconLit export was used.

What the outcome groups mean +
Workplace output · 31
Observed productivity, sales, innovation, safety, time use and other operational output. The category includes nonfinancial outcomes and is broader than firm profit.
Employment outcomes · 9
Retention, absence, hiring, labor supply and workforce composition. These do not by themselves establish profits or productivity.
Rated/proximal performance · 10
Rated or incompletely verified performance, creativity, mastery and related proximal outcomes.
Adjacent evidence · 18
AI/work design, public-service production, entrepreneur training and laboratory/professional task evidence. Relevant, but different from ordinary employee HR interventions and realized private-business returns.
How to read a causal claim +

Randomization identifies an assignment effect in the studied setting. It does not automatically identify effects among participants, mediation or transportability to your organization.

Difference-in-differences needs credible counterfactual trends. Instrumental variables need relevance and exclusion. Fixed effects remove stable confounding only. Matching and machine-learning adjustment still depend on observed confounders and overlap. Selection corrections depend on exclusion and/or functional-form assumptions.

“Other experiment / allocation unverified” avoids assuming randomization from the word “experiment.” Practice groups are interpretive navigation labels, not statistically discovered clusters.

Why there is no pooled effect chart +

The papers estimate different quantities: sales changes, exit hazards, time allocation, ratings, patent novelty and more. Even percentages do not necessarily have a common meaning. Averaging them would produce a misleading number.

Reported estimates are selective point estimates, not standardized effects. Confidence intervals and specification variants were not comprehensively extracted. A missing estimate means “not extracted,” not zero. Subgroup findings, hazard ratios and one-standard-deviation treatment changes remain labeled.

Journal coverage +

A curated roster selected for prominence and relevance across I/O psychology, HR, management/organization and economics. No numerical impact-factor cutoff was imposed. Complete metadata retrieval is not proof of complete indexing or screening.

Exclusions & open questions +

38 important near misses, date exclusions and candidates needing further method checks are recorded below. “Full methods needed” does not mean that a paper lacks a causal design.

What would make this more complete +

An expanded prespecified journal roster; licensed database exports and publisher online-first tables of contents; complete independent title/abstract and full-text screening; citation chasing; formal risk-of-bias and estimand extraction; and verification of uncertain publication dates.

A journal with no retained papers is not evidence that it published no eligible research. Crossref deposits can be missing or wrong, title filters miss unusual labels, and paywalls limit inspection.
